Bri4Dge: Bridging Agent Reasoning with World Model Prediction through 4D Point Steering

Anonymous authors
Paper under review
Generated video Generated 4D points

A world model predicts video and 4D points. An agent edits the points. The model regenerates the plan before the robot moves.

Abstract

Video world models predict the consequences of robot actions, but their predictions are difficult to inspect and correct through pixels alone. We present a world model that exposes its predicted future as metric 3D point trajectories over time, i.e. 4D points, providing an editable geometric plan. The model jointly generates video and point trajectories using a pretrained video diffusion transformer and decodes robot actions solely from the point latent. A vision-language agent inspects the generated trajectories alongside current observations, identifies anticipated task failures, and edits the relevant points. The world model then regenerates video and point trajectories from the edited plan and decodes actions from the regenerated point latent. This connects geometric corrections to both future prediction and action generation. In simulation, agent steering increases average success from 20.8% to 47.5% on four FurnitureBench assembly tasks and from 7.7% to 27.4% on RoboCasa365 composite tasks. These results show that an explicit metric plan lets agent reasoning correct anticipated failures before execution, with the largest gains on long tasks whose later steps depend on earlier ones.
